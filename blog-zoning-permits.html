Understanding Dallas ADU Zoning Laws & Permits

Navigating the zoning laws and permit requirements for building an ADU in Dallas can feel overwhelming. The City of Dallas has specific regulations governing accessory dwelling units, and understanding these rules is essential before starting your project. In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down everything you need to know about Dallas ADU regulations.

What is an ADU According to Dallas Regulations?

In Dallas, an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) is defined as a secondary housing unit on a single-family residential lot. ADUs can be:

  • Detached: A separate structure in your backyard (casita, cottage, or granny flat)
  • Attached: An addition connected to your main house
  • Converted: A garage, basement, or attic conversion
  • Internal: A portion of the existing home converted to a separate unit

Dallas ADU Zoning Requirements (2025)

Here are the key zoning requirements for ADUs in Dallas:

Size Restrictions

  • Maximum ADU size: 1,000 square feet or 40% of the main home's living area (whichever is less)
  • Minimum ADU size: No official minimum, but typically 200+ sq ft for livability
  • Maximum height: Generally 2 stories or 25 feet for detached ADUs

Setback Requirements

  • Rear setback: Typically 5 feet from property line
  • Side setback: Usually 5 feet minimum
  • Distance from main house: Varies by district
  • Front setback: ADUs generally not permitted in front yards

Lot Requirements

  • Minimum lot size requirements vary by zoning district
  • Lot coverage limits must be maintained (typically 45-50%)
  • Must maintain adequate open space on property

"Dallas has been progressively updating its ADU regulations to encourage more housing options. Working with an experienced local contractor ensures you're building according to the latest codes and requirements."

The Dallas ADU Permit Process

Getting permits for your Dallas ADU involves several steps:

  1. Pre-Application Meeting (Optional but Recommended): Meet with the City Planning Department to discuss your project and confirm zoning compliance.
  2. Site Plan Submission: Submit a detailed site plan showing the ADU location, setbacks, and lot coverage calculations.
  3. Building Plans: Submit complete architectural and structural plans prepared by a licensed professional.
  4. Plan Review: City reviews plans for building code compliance (typically 2-4 weeks).
  5. Permit Issuance: Once approved, permits are issued and construction can begin.
  6. Inspections: Multiple inspections during construction (foundation, framing, electrical, plumbing, final).
  7. Certificate of Occupancy: Final approval allowing legal occupancy of the ADU.

Permit Costs in Dallas

Expect to pay the following permit-related fees for your Dallas ADU project:

  • Building Permit: $1,500 - $4,000 (based on project value)
  • Plan Review: $500 - $1,500
  • Electrical Permit: $200 - $500
  • Plumbing Permit: $200 - $500
  • Mechanical (HVAC) Permit: $200 - $400
  • Impact Fees: Varies by location
  • Total Estimated Permit Costs: $3,000 - $8,000

Common ADU Permit Challenges in Dallas

Here are some common issues that can delay or complicate the permit process:

  • HOA Restrictions: Some HOAs have additional restrictions or prohibitions on ADUs. Check your HOA rules first.
  • Historic Districts: Properties in historic districts face additional review requirements.
  • Utility Capacity: Some areas may require utility upgrades before ADU approval.
  • Floodplain Issues: Properties in flood zones face additional requirements.
  • Septic Systems: Properties with septic (not city sewer) may need system upgrades.
